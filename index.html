First Aid

What to do until help arrives.

Quick, general first aid steps for common emergencies. Tap a card to expand it.

Always call your local emergency number first (911, 999, 112, etc.) in a serious emergency. These steps are general guidance to help while you wait for responders — they don't replace professional medical care, and LifeTap is not a medical device or emergency service.

  1. Ask "Are you choking?" If they can cough, speak, or breathe, encourage them to keep coughing.
  2. If they can't breathe, speak, or are making a high-pitched sound, call emergency services (or have someone else call).
  3. Stand behind them, lean them forward, and give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  4. If that doesn't clear it, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich): fist above the navel, other hand over it, pull sharply inward and upward.
  5. Repeat back blows and abdominal thrusts until the object is dislodged, help arrives, or the person becomes unresponsive — then start CPR.
  1. Check responsiveness — tap and shout. If no response, call emergency services immediately (or direct someone to).
  2. Check for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. If they're not breathing normally, start CPR.
  3. Lay them on a firm, flat surface. Kneel beside their chest.
  4. Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest, other hand on top, fingers interlaced.
  5. Push hard and fast — about 5–6 cm (2 inches) deep, at 100–120 compressions per minute (roughly the beat of "Stayin' Alive").
  6. Allow the chest to fully recoil between compressions. If trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions.
  7. If you're not trained or not comfortable with breaths, continue hands-only compressions until help arrives or an AED is available.
  8. If an AED is available, turn it on and follow its voice prompts.
  1. Call emergency services for any severe or spurting bleed.
  2. If possible, use gloves or a barrier to protect yourself from blood contact.
  3. Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth, dressing, or your hand.
  4. Keep pressing — don't lift the cloth to check, add more layers on top if it soaks through.
  5. If possible, raise the injured area above the level of the heart.
  6. If bleeding is life-threatening and on a limb, and direct pressure isn't controlling it, apply a tourniquet a few inches above the wound (not on a joint), note the time it was applied, and don't loosen it until medical help takes over.
  7. Keep the person warm and lying down, and monitor for signs of shock (pale, cold, clammy skin, rapid breathing, confusion).
  1. Move the person away from the heat source. Remove jewelry/tight clothing near the burn before swelling starts (unless stuck to skin).
  2. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 10–20 minutes.
  3. Cover loosely with a clean, non-fluffy dressing or cling film — don't wrap tightly.
  4. Don't apply ice, butter, oil, or ointments, and don't burst any blisters.
  5. Call emergency services for large or deep burns, burns on the face/hands/genitals, chemical or electrical burns, or burns on infants and young children.

  3. This keeps the airway open and lets fluids drain rather than being inhaled.
  4. Monitor their breathing continuously until help arrives — be ready to start CPR if breathing stops.
  5. Keep them warm, and don't give them anything to eat or drink.

  2. A — Arms: Ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift downward?
  3. S — Speech: Ask them to repeat a simple phrase. Is it slurred or strange?
  4. T — Time: If any of these signs are present, call emergency services immediately and note the time symptoms started — this matters for treatment.
  5. Keep the person calm, sitting or lying with head/shoulders slightly raised, and don't give them food or drink (risk of choking).

  4. Once the jerking stops, roll them onto their side (recovery position) and check breathing.
  5. Call emergency services if the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, they don't regain consciousness, another seizure follows immediately, they're injured, or it's their first known seizure.

  3. If there's an open wound with bone visible, cover it loosely with a clean dressing without pressing on the bone, and control any bleeding around it.
  4. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling, if available.
